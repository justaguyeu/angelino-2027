Zanzibar beaches: discover the perfect spot for your vacation

Zanzibar beaches: discover the perfect spot for your vacation

23658
Reading time: 13 min.
Islands

Many travelers come to Tanzania for its world-famous safaris and the challenge of climbing Mount Kilimanjaro. After days of adventure and exploration, though, many look to unwind, making the Zanzibar beach holidays the perfect choice. Visitors are drawn to the clear waters of the Indian Ocean, luxury hotels, and the range of water sports available, including snorkeling and kitesurfing.

This article provides a detailed selection of the best beaches in Zanzibar, catering to different preferences, from popular spots to those perfect for active or secluded getaways.

Top 3 most popular beaches in Zanzibar

Nungwi Beach

Nungwi Beach is located in the northern part of Unguja, the archipelago's main island. It is renowned as one of the most popular tourist destinations. The area bustles with bars and restaurants serving fresh seafood, featuring live music, and offering plenty of entertainment. Known for its white sandy shores and crystal-clear turquoise waters, Nungwi Beach also boasts breathtaking crimson sunsets.

Nungwi Beach from a bird's eye view. Photo source: stock.adobe.com

One key advantage here is that the tides are barely noticeable. For instance, in the western part of the beach, the water recedes only about 25 meters (80 feet) during low tide. In contrast, the beaches of Chwaka and Charawe, which are less suitable for tourism, can see the water pull back as far as 1.5 to 2 kilometers (0.9 to 1.2 miles) at low tide.

Later in the article, we will explain why this happens and how to choose a beach based on the tide features. For now, it’s worth mentioning that Nungwi is also ideal for solo travelers looking to connect with others for activities. During the high season, this beach is bustling with visitors. Diving centers are nearby, and the coral reefs make Nungwi’s coastal waters an excellent spot for scuba diving and snorkeling.

Kendwa Beach

Kendwa Beach is often compared to Nungwi but offers a calmer and less crowded atmosphere. The two beaches connect during low tide, allowing visitors to stroll along the sand from one end to the other. Like Nungwi, Kendwa Beach is unaffected by strong tides that could disrupt your vacation. The water here is clean and crystal clear, making it an ideal spot for diving, snorkeling, or simply swimming in the warm rays of the African sun.

Kendwa Beach is an oasis for peaceful and serene relaxation. Photo source: Tripadvisor

Kendwa Beach is also a popular hub for vibrant nightlife, known for its lively parties, restaurants, and bars. One of the most popular events is the Full Moon Party, which occurs monthly at the Kendwa Rocks Hotel.

Bwejuu Beach

This stretch of soft white sand on the island's southeast coast is just a 90-minute drive from Stone Town on the west coast. Nestled among lush green palm trees and comfortably away from bustling tourist hotspots, this location is ideal for a tranquil and secluded escape.

Bwejuu Beach is surrounded by palm trees. Photo source: Tripadvisor

There are plenty of activities to enjoy near the beach. You can stroll through the mangrove forests, visit the nearby village to immerse yourself in the local culture, or take a trip to Stone Town or Jozani Forest, which is home to many colobus monkeys.

Top 3 best beaches in Zanzibar for snorkeling and active leisure

Paje Beach

Paje Beach, located on the southeast coast of Zanzibar Island, is particularly popular with kitesurfing enthusiasts. It provides ideal sports conditions, featuring consistent winds and shallow waters along the shoreline.

Azure waters of Paje Beach. Photo source: istockphoto.com

Zanzibar has two tourist seasons: from January to February and from July to September. However, the best time for kitesurfing in Paje Beach is from June to October and from December to March. During these months, the wind blows consistently across the shore, creating ideal conditions that are particularly comfortable for beginners learning the sport.  

Additionally, this beach has a separate lagoon with crystal-clear, wave-free water that is home to unique coral formations. These corals attract a variety of colorful tropical fish, making it a dream destination for snorkeling enthusiasts.

Pwani Mchangani Beach

The beach is located on the northeast coast of the island, just under 50 km (30 miles) from Stone Town. It is the perfect coastline for kitesurfing.

Kitesurfing is a popular recreational activity at Pwani Mchangani. Photo source: Tripadvisor

Despite the significant tides at the beach, there is a remarkable reef located about 1.5 kilometers (1 mile) from the shore, known locally as "the aquarium." During low tide, you can walk out to it and observe the vibrant marine life. A large crowd of people serves as the main landmark to help you find the spot.

In addition to exploring the reef, there are plenty of other activities available, including quad biking, off-roading, fishing, and scuba diving.

Top 3 best beaches in Zanzibar for a private getaway

Matemwe Beach

Matemwe Beach is situated on the northeastern coast of Zanzibar. It boasts white sand, crystal-clear waters, and a serene, meditative atmosphere. It’s the perfect spot for a peaceful retreat away from the busy tourist areas and loud parties. The coral reefs in this area make it an ideal location for scuba diving, while the vibrant sunsets over the Indian Ocean create unforgettable memories. The tides here are more pronounced than in the northern part of the island, but they're still easy to handle.  

Tourists often describe Matemwe Beach as having miles of deserted beach, warm ocean waters, and abundant starfish, but few cafes are nearby.

Pemba

One of the most beautiful and still secluded islands in the archipelago, Pemba is the perfect destination for a tranquil getaway away from noisy beach parties and large crowds of tourists.

In terms of size, Pemba is less than half the area of Zanzibar, and it can only be accessed by ferry or local airline flights from Arusha, Dar es Salaam, and a few other tourist towns in the country. The ferry to the island runs only twice a week and takes about five hours. Flying is much faster and more convenient: the flight from Zanzibar Island to Pemba takes around 30 minutes.

Pristine beaches of Pemba Island. Photo source: Tripadvisor

Most of the island is covered in dense jungles, and the main civilized center is a small town called Chake Chake. There are only a handful of hotels on Pemba. Many locals still wear traditional clothing and primarily speak Swahili.

One of the most significant advantages of visiting Pemba Island is its ideal conditions for diving. Unlike Zanzibar, where depths rarely exceed 60 to 70 meters (200 to 230 feet), Pemba boasts much deeper waters, reaching up to 1.5 kilometers (1 mile). This makes it an excellent spot for encountering various fascinating marine life. For diving, we recommend Afro Divers, a dive center located right on the beach and surrounded by majestic baobab trees, away from the busy tourist areas. The center is run by a young couple from the Netherlands and France, and they provide everything you need for a safe and enjoyable diving experience.

These cozy, comfortable cottages are situated in some of the most picturesque spots on the island, and the on-site restaurant serves dishes made from the freshest ingredients. Guests often mention the excellent service, friendly staff, and top-notch spa services. However, there is one caveat: be prepared for the possibility of weak Wi-Fi in certain areas of the island, regardless of where you stay.

Tides on Zanzibar beaches

Tides are crucial and can greatly affect your Zanzibar beach holiday experience. Knowing which beaches experience significant water withdrawal during low tide and which have minimal differences is essential.

For example, the difference during low tide is nearly invisible on the northern beaches. Moderate tides are also common along the east coast. In contrast, the most significant withdrawal of coastal waters occurs in Chwaka Bay, making it less ideal for beachgoers.

Low tides on the east coast leave a lot of seaweed and sea urchins on the beach. Because of this, it's advisable not to walk barefoot in this area. We recommend bringing special coral shoes (also known as coral slippers or aqua shoes) for your trip.

Tides are caused by the moon's gravitational pull on the surface of the world's oceans. The highest tides occur during the new and full moon when the sun's influence enhances the moon's gravitational force. The time difference between the lowest points of the water level is approximately 12 hours, meaning that about six hours pass between high and low tides. Over 24 hours, there are typically two low tides and two high tides.

The terrain can affect how tides behave, creating differences in water levels even on nearby beaches or parts of the same beach. Moreover, the waters of the open ocean are more susceptible to significant high and low tides than those in bays.

During your stay in Zanzibar, it's advisable to plan each day around the tide schedule. Some websites and apps allow you to track the current water levels and check the schedule for changes at different times. Here are some helpful online resources that provide up-to-date information:

  • Tidetime.org
  • Tide-forecast.com
  • Tideschart.com

Please note that the timing of each tide shifts forward by 50 minutes each day. This change is due to the Earth's daily rotation around the sun and the moon's complete orbit around the Earth. For instance, if the tide occurs at 9 AM today, it will take place at 9:50 AM tomorrow and at 10:40 AM the following day, and so forth.
